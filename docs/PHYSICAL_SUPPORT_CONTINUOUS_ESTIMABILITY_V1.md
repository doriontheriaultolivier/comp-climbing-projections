# Continuous estimability profile for physical support V1

This audit converts the sparse-data methods decision into a 23-profile evidence
inventory. It does not fit a physical ceiling or turn any count into a release
gate.

For every physical protocol and board metric it reports distinct athletes,
repeats, linked observations, future event contexts, event/athlete concentration,
time coverage and pending item-candidate coverage. Effective clusters are the
inverse Herfindahl index of linked-row shares. They diagnose concentration; they
are not a claim that repeated rows are independent competitions.

Examples illustrate why binary sample rules are inappropriate:

- Kilter 50%-flash: 97 observations, 21 athletes, 15 with repeats, 94 linked
  observations and 142 future event contexts.
- Kilter recent-hardest: 74 observations, 19 athletes, 14 with repeats, 71
  linked observations and 128 future event contexts. Exposure remains
  unidentified without session/attempt history.
- 20mm RFD: 9 observations, 7 athletes, 2 with repeats and 65 linked event
  contexts. This supports heavy shrinkage and sensitivity analysis, not an
  athlete-specific conclusion.
- L-sit: one observation from one athlete and five linked event contexts, with
  one cluster carrying a third of linked rows. Any estimated effect would be
  predominantly assumption-driven.

Across the 635 input observations, 613 link chronologically to later results.
Pending item candidates cover most Boulder-linked rows, but completed human
demand-tag coverage remains zero. Candidate availability is not tag evidence.

The externally reviewed model ladder remains:

1. frozen chronological baseline and concentration audit;
2. after independent tags, a strongly shrunk monotone additive Zone and
   Top-given-Zone support challenger;
3. CES/soft-min only if compensation parameters become identifiable and stable;
4. a one-sided access/frontier component only after repeated matched
   observations separate access variation from symmetric error.

Every future result must report prior sensitivity, athlete/event influence,
missingness sensitivity and grouped chronological proper scores. Sparse evidence
widens and destabilizes the conclusion; it does not become zero or fail merely
because a fixed count was missed.
